Gameplay Overview

[edit]

Rock Howard is an all rounder with a simple, but versatile and effective toolkit.

With Rock, what you see is what you get. He has all of the standard issue tools one needs to be successful in City of the Wolves, including a good projectile, a good DP, a good corner-carry special, and good use of REV Gauge. His Far C is a great poking and combo tool, he has great buttons for enforcing hop pressure, and he hurts quite a lot. If you're familiar with the basics of a shoto archetype, Rock has you covered.

Rock does have a number of trickier tools up his sleeve, however. Alongside Reppuu Ken, his grounded projectile, Rock also has Shippuu Ken, an aerial projectile that halts his movement. His Rage Run command dash can lead into crossups or same side mixups, while his Shinkuu Nage command grab leads to combos on both regular and REV hits. All of these give Rock even more versatility, which synergies nicely with his plethora of defensive options. Great mashable lights, a strong DP, a counter, a good reversal super, and a command grab for Guard Cancelling make Rock hard to crack open.

Rock's got it all, but it doesn't mean he's got it the best. While all of his tools are good, none of them are top tier and all have specific weaknesses. If you want zoning, you're better off playing dedicated zoners, and ditto for rushdown characters. Still, Rock is designed to play COTW well, and that he does. He's an easy recommend to anyone just starting the game, and legacy SNK veterans who want to flex their fundamentals.
Pros Cons
  • Strong Normals: With strong pokes such as 2B and f.C, good jump-ins with j.C and j.D, and plus frames that can be easily linked on hit to 2C, Rock has moves that can fit almost any need.
  • Varied Defense: Rock can use his Crack Counter (236B/D), A Rising Tackle (623A), or Raging Storm (236236A/C) either on wakeup or buffered after a Just Defend to keep opponents guessing, or he can rely on his strong lights to mash out as well.
  • Strike / Throw: Not only is his strike/throw with his normal grab bolstered by his well-rounded normals, Rock can also use Shinkuu Nage (632146A/C) to side switch or get extra damage with the REV version. Shinkuu Nage being available from Rage Run (214B/D) also makes his mixups in Rage Run even scarier. Rock also has a strong 50/50 mixup between letting a meaty Low Dodge Attack rock for an overhead or kara canceling it into Shinkuu Nage, making his knockdowns and setups that much more powerful.
  • Zoning: For a character that is supposed to be well rounded. Rock has extremely good zoning with both his Reppuu Ken and Shippuu Ken. REV Shippuu Ken is in the running for one of the best moves in the game as it covers so much air and ground space for relatively small risk. Rock can also counter zone using his Heavy and REV Reppuu Ken, his Redline Shining Knuckle, and his ground REV Blow.
  • Lackluster Anti-Airs: With a wonky High Dodge Attack that has poor vertical range and often moves Rock under his opponent's jump-ins and 2C's habit of trading, Rock has to rely on his C Rising Tackle Brake to anti-air consistently, which is more committal than other characters with better High Dodge Attacks.

The Wild Punisher.

Rock does a somewhat slow side kick, It’s a decent long-range poke but can only be super cancelled, with practice you can hit-confirm into a buffered Shining Knuckle. On block this move deals good REV damage, but its main use is for Wild Punishing. Combined with f.C’s range and special cancel, it’s easy to follow up with a combo at mid-range after a Wild Punish by running in and doing f.C — however, at farther ranges you may need to dash more in order to connect with f.C and you might not be able to do so if you're too far away. In these situations, Rock can instead opt to just link into REV Hard Edge and forgo the run into f.C.
