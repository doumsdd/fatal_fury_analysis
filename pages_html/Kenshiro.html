Gameplay Overview

[edit]

An iconic manga and anime hero who arguably doesn’t need an introduction, CotW has managed to recreate Kenshiro’s ruthless, brutal fighting style perfectly. In this game, he’s a powerful infighter who can pressure and dish out punishment like no one else in the roster, provided he can get up close where he’s most potent.

It’s the close-range prowess that makes Kenshiro really stand out. He’s second to none when it comes to confirming, pressuring, pushing strike-throw, high-low or even left-right mix, just about anything that involves erasing the opponent’s health bar and doing it quickly. He accomplishes such high damage by activating his opponents’ channeling points with his REV moves, removing their ability to Just Defend and enhancing the next REV move or super that lands on them. With the debuff enabling such crazy offensive potential, by the time opponents know what hit them, odds are that they’re already dead.

Unfortunately, with most of his moveset dedicated to close-range combat, Kenshiro really can’t do much until he’s up close. While he has multiple tools to answer to projectiles, they don’t help him move in at all, only buy him time to find an opening. As a result, getting in on keen, passive opponents as Kenshiro can be quite the hassle, and in many cases, a strenuous test of patience.

As many will flock to Kenshiro just because of his iconic status, he’ll shine the brightest in the hands of players who patiently wait for their chance to move in and unleash an unpredictable beatdown. Whether you’re a Fist of the North Star fan, or a fighting game veteran, chances are you’ll enjoy playing Kenshiro and piloting the devastating power of Hokuto Shinken.

Kenshiro is a high-pressure infighter centered around close-range offense and a myriad of striking moves.
Pros Cons
  • Close-Range Powerhouse: No one is scarier when they have the advantage than Kenshiro. He’s damn near got it all; a comboable overhead that he can fake, good lows, left-right mix, frame traps, stagger pressure, super strong hit confirms, and more. Better yet, all of the above leads to combos or plus frames, letting Kenshiro keep going crazy for as long as his REV meter will allow.
  • Debuff: Kenshiro’s REV attacks apply a debuff that takes away his opponent’s ability to Just Defend and enhances the next REV move or super that hits. With Just Defending off the table whenever opponents are debuffed, Kenshiro can safely pour on the pressure with no fear of a rebuttal via Guard Cancel.
  • Super High Damage: Kenshiro’s combo damage is absurdly high, off of any hit. On top of the debuff’s repeated application in combos letting Kenshiro put together ridiculous, high-damage punishment, he has links from lights to 5AC that let him seamlessly move into stronger sequences, turning lower-commitment buttons into potentially round-winning damage.
  • Offensive Variation: Kenshiro’s offense is not just about what he hits with, but what he doesn’t, as well. The feint on his overhead and the brake on his Finger Snap of Emptiness can turn opponents into sitting ducks blocking the wrong way amd taking big damage as punishment.
  • Side-Switching DP: It’s not enough that Kenshiro has an invincible DP; it switches sides on hit, too. Landing it when he’s cornered can instantly swing the tide of battle in his favor, escaping the corner while leaving his opponent with little breathing room.
  • Limited From a Distance: With most of his special moves focused on raw offense and enforcing guessing games, Kenshiro is largely ineffective from outside the range of his normals. While he has answers to fireballs, they don’t advance him forward at all, meaning he still has to work his way in to be a threat.
  • Easily REV Guarded: Kenshiro is exceedingly weak against REV Guard, which denies his left-right mixup and causes blocked moves to push the opponent back out of his range. Even REV Accel isn’t guaranteed pressure, as REV Hundred-Kick Rush is easily Hyper Defended.
  • Subpar Midscreen Okizeme: The constant side switching on Kenshiro’s REV Accel combos and his DPs means he may struggle to corner opponents unless he leaves damage on the table or stuns his opponent. Combined with his lack of hard knockdowns without super or REV Blow, Kenshiro’s okizeme can be quite lacking without resources.
  • Loses Even More in Overheat: Just like Mr. Karate, Kenshiro loses more than the average character when Overheated. On top of his DP losing invincibility, being Overheated stops Kenshiro from applying or triggering the debuff without spending meter. This also makes his reversal supers much less threatening.


About Kenshiro’s Debuff and the Stun State

[edit]

Debuff: Activated Channeling Points

[edit]

Kenshiro’s REV Arts, REV Blows, and Hokuto Fist of Penitence super apply a special debuff on hit, where the opponent’s “channeling points” are activated. Debuffed opponents sparkle with a blue lightning effect. The debuff lasts for six seconds if Kenshiro does not trigger its special effect.

  • Debuffed opponents cannot Just Defend nor Hyper Defend. They can still REV Guard to avoid chip damage.
  • When a REV Art, REV Blow, or Ignition/Redline Gear hits a debuffed opponent, it triggers the debuff, granting additional effects to the move that triggered it.
  • Kenshiro can apply and trigger the debuff multiple times in a combo.
  • The debuff state does not go away if Kenshiro is hit. Unless Kenshiro triggers the debuff effect, it will only go away after six seconds have passed.

Stun State

[edit]

Kenshiro can briefly stun an opponent by hitting them with an Ignition or Redline Gear (super) while the debuff is on them. The stun effect is similar to other fighting games that have a stun/dizzy mechanic such as Street Fighter where the opponent is totally helpless, though it only lasts for around 2 full seconds and requires Kenshiro to be quick if he wants to follow up on it with a combo.

  • The Ignition Gear version of Thousand-Hand Crushing Fist will only stun a debuffed opponent if they're grounded. If Kenshiro hits a debuffed opponent with this move while they're airborne they will instead be launched upward as if they were hit by a special move Counter Hit, allowing for other followup options.
  • The Redline Gear version of Hokuto Fist of Penitence will stun the opponent regardless of whether or not they are debuffed.
