Gameplay Overview

[edit]

Joe is an aggressive all-rounder, with emphasis on the “aggressive” part — he really shines up close where he can go HAM with seemingly endless pressure and mix-ups.

Joe has a nice set of moves that help him navigate neutral, like a fireball that is massively plus if blocked at range, and an invincible DP to ward off aggression so he can engage on his terms. Once he’s in, it’s party time; on top of high combo damage, he has numerous ways to pressure and chip away at opponents’ health while staying on the offensive, from grounded crossups to spacing traps. If that’s not enough to open up an opponent, he can tap into Fearless Man, a stance with guard point that can be followed up with a low, overhead, or even a command grab. From nearly hit Joe lands, even light confirms, he can use REV Accel to convert to higher damage, often coupled with great okizeme to stay on the attack.

Strong as he is up close, Joe’s mid-range tools can be quite risky because his far normals either are very slow or don’t cancel to specials, and said specials are unusually high-commitment, even with their Brakes. Joe isn’t much of a threat from afar, either, due to underwhelming fireballs and no long-range punishment whatsoever, not even with a super. Against patient opponents, winning neutral often requires taking big gambles that other characters don’t have to take.

All in all, Joe is for the opportunistic players who like to carefully look for their openings, then use those opportunities to mount a relentless assault. He’s not as easy to play as his shoto-esque moveset implies, but when he gets his chance, he can rock opponents like a hurricane!

Joe is a offensive monster disguised as an all-rounder, boasting strong pressure, mix-ups and conversions backed up by a capable neutral game.
Pros Cons
  • Close-Range Menace: Joe is undeniably strong up close. He has access to a variety of mixup tools for lows, highs, right/left, and a command grab, and that’s not even mentioning the great frame data on his close moves. Joe also has one of the fastest dashes in the game which makes his offense even scarier
  • Dynamic Neutral: Joe can play from a variety of ranges; harassing the opponent from midscreen with hurricanes and tiger kicks one moment and barging in with an advancing special the next.
  • Fearless Man: Joe’s new stance is terrifying. With its guard point and follow-ups including a command grab and projectile reflector, Joe can use this stance to easily punish an opponent for being predictable. With REV to spare, Joe can even protect himself from lows, or use REV Accel to combo off one of its followups.
  • Light Confirms: Thanks to REV Tiger Kick, Joe easily converts sequences of two or even three lights into a variety of combos, giving him much more combo freedom than other characters.
  • Glasses: They make him look good.
  • Struggles Against Zoning: On top of having no safe advancing special from half-screen, all of Joe’s projectiles have horrible frame data and are not good in fireball wars, especially the REV version which is comically bad for anti-zoning. While he can reflect fireballs with Tiger Tempest, that’s time spent at long range where he’ll continue to be unthreatening until he moves in.
  • Risky Mid-Range: Joe’s far normals are all very underwhelming, being slow, stubby, unable to cancel to special moves, or a combination between the three. This leaves his mid-range game very reliant on his committal special moves.
  • High REV Dependence: Joe is very meter hungry, using it to make stance mixups safer, gain plus frames, and access high damage combo routes.
  • Some People Don’t Like Glasses: That’s unfortunate.
