Gameplay Overview

[edit]

A memorable villain who last appeared in KOF '98 UM, Wolfgang Krauser is a well-rounded bully character who is defined by his screen presence, big damage, and ability to completely overwhelm his opponent.

Krauser has a bit of an unusual moveset, with fireballs reminiscent of Sagat's Tiger Shot, an anti-air grab, armored command grab, a pair of advancing specials, and long-reaching normals, which seems simple enough on paper. But where they truly shine are in their collective ability to completely overpower opponents' responses to them. Krauser can charge his fireballs to simply beat opponents' projectiles, his anti-air grab can't be Just Defended, and above-average damage on his normals means he simply wins trades; alas, challenging Krauser directly is tantamount to chiseling your own gravestone. If Krauser gets a clean hit, he can hit incredibly hard with his high combo damage, ending with a hard knockdown to continue his assault, whether it be up close or even from afar with a charged Blitz Ball or Kaiser Wave.

Krauser may thus seem like an unstoppable force at first, but look closer and you will see this emperor's flaws. None of his special moves are invincible instantly, forcing him to rely on his valuable meter for an invincible reversal. As a character on the slow side with poor reward from his lows, he lacks the ability to stop an opponent from moving around the screen to evade his big attacks. This stacks with being a big body, giving faster characters ample opportunity to strike if he commits to one of his bigger attacks.

All in all, Krauser is a solid character, perfect for players who enjoy simply overpowering their adversaries. With his simple game plan, easy-to-execute combos and dominant neutral, he's also a great choice for new players.

Krauser is a brutish all-rounder who can overpower opponents anywhere on the screen with his long reach, high damage, and unique moves.
Pros Cons
  • Omnipresent: Krauser is a constant threat from anywhere on the screen. He can zone with fast fireballs from long range, overpower other characters' projectiles with his own, duke it out in mid-range with his massive normals, pressure the opponent up close with his multiple tools, and everything in between, and in all of the above cases, he has tools to simply overpower the opponent's response.
  • Absurd Damage: Krauser's combo damage is absolutely massive, with light-to-heavy links, above-average damage on many normals and special moves, and intense counter hit routes; it's possible to hit 700 damage in a combo even on a normal hit.
  • Unstoppable Anti-Air: Krauser is the only character in the game whose main anti-air option cannot be Just Defended. As an anti-air grab, Kaiser Claw (623P) prevents the opponent from resorting to the simple answer of jumping over his fireballs or normals, as doing so puts them in a hard knockdown.
  • Says No to REV Blow: With an air-invincible grab, a command grab with guard point, good light strings, and a reasonably quick super, Krauser has an easy time shutting down REV Blows, even outside of S.P.G.
  • REV Gauge Buildup: Krauser has multiple ways to drive up the opponent's REV Gauge when he has the advantage, with multiple plus-on-block specials and long-reaching moves, not to mention a hard knockdown into charged Kaiser Wave putting a whopping 70% REV buildup or more on the opponent's wakeup.
  • No Meterless Reversal: Krauser's only invincible reversals are Gigantic Cyclone and his Hidden Gear, meaning he will struggle to deal with pressure and mix-ups. While his unique command grab has fast guard point to break frame traps, the grab itself is too slow to be a reliable defensive option on top of being a high risk low reward option.
  • Unrewarding Lows: Krauser's only low starter is his 2B and he has a singular hyperspecific way to convert into REV moves from it, making his low presence usually very underwhelming without a conversion into super.
  • Somewhat Slow: Krauser doesn't move very quickly; his walk and dash speeds are slower than your average character. More nimble characters can throw him off his game as he can't quickly reposition to keep opponents where he wants them.
  • Vulnerable to Whiff Punishes: The hurtboxes on Krauser's longer pokes stay out for a while on whiff, leaving him more vulnerable to Wild Punishes than many other characters.

  • All versions hit OTG

This super has a lot of utility and is potentially one of the strongest supers in the game. Extremely combo-friendly and easy to link to.

Despite looking quite gimmicky, this super is EXTREMELY HARD to deal with even outside of combos, due to its ability to be held to avoid Just Defense. Oftentimes, the opponent will have to choose between blocking the hit and suffering the REV buildup, simply taking the hit to protect their REV, or try to challenge it and potentially get hit. However, this super is extremely minus on block at all ranges and has more recovery the longer it is charged. Punishing Kaiser Wave on block depends on the range and character-specific tools. Marco's Haou Shoukou Ken, Rock's Shining Knuckle, Terry's Buster Wolf, and Preecha's P=Mc^2 are all great and fairly consistent punish options if the projectile was not blocked from completely full screen.

Notably, this super can be held to boost its properties. After being held for 45 frames, the super becomes purple, and after being held for 120 frames, it will become red. It can be held for up to 225 frames (Ignition) / 229 frames (Redline) before Krauser releases it automatically. When held for at least 45 frames, Kaiser Wave can no longer be Just Defended, gains increased damage depending on charge length, has massively increased REV buildup (70%/85% for purple, 75%/95% for red) and completely beats out other supers of the same strength (Rock's Raging Storm, Marco's Haoh Shoukou Ken, etc.) unless they have projectile invincibility.

Almost all of Krauser's hard knockdowns give him time to charge Kaiser Wave this long, forcing the opponent to potentially make a difficult decision between getting hit or getting Overheated.
