Gameplay Overview

[edit]

Nightmare Geese has a devious game plan befitting of the villainy of his past life. At first, he looks like a zoner, with best-in-class fireballs and impressive-reaching normals. These give him something of a defensive, condition-based neutral game.

In truth, where Geese truly shines is when the opponent caves and gives him a chance to punish them. It’s here where the nightmare begins, as Geese has some of the deepest variation of offense in the game. He’s got lows, overheads, frame traps, plus-frame resets, a command grab, fast Wild Punishers, and more, and with REV to spare, he can combo from all of the above. When the opponent makes a desperate attempt to escape this suffocating offensive, Geese can shut down counterattacks instantly with his iconic counters, or punish poor use of reversals with some of the game’s highest combo damage, ending with a hard knockdown to either continue his offense, or retreat behind the fireball game to defend his lead.

If the opponent doesn’t give in to Geese’s strong neutral game, he isn’t that scary. Geese has virtually no answers to jump-ins, as he is severely lacking in reliable anti-airs. Potent as his offense is, he has no gap-closer outside of his REV Blow, which hinders his access to his damage and momentum. Thus Geese has a hard time getting his offense started on his terms, and with enough patience, opponents can exploit his poor defense and turn this nightmare on itself.

Getting to that point against Geese isn’t easy, though, especially when you know how to use his potent neutral weapons. Once you get the hang of them, all it takes is one or two favorable interactions, and those yellow-bellies will DIE FOREVER.

Nightmare Geese is a balanced character who uses his strong neutral control to goad the opponent into opening themselves up for huge punishment.
Pros Cons
  • Mid-Range Threat: Geese can control space easily with his advancing far heavies (with far C being cancellable), his long reaching and cancellable Far B and a long low in the form of 2B, and can counter-poke with his command normal 5AC. Using his long reaching and hard-hitting normals, Nightmare Geese is a poking and whiff punish machine.
  • Nightmarish Combos: Geese boasts some of the highest overall combo damage in the game, easily capable of hitting the 700-damage benchmark midscreen. He can two-touch opponents without spending a bar of meter, and should he score a Wild Punish off his Far D, he's likely to deal round-winning damage. Not to mention, should his opponent survive, they are likely cornered through the immense corner carry in his combos.
  • Hard Knockdowns: Unusual for a non-grappling character, Geese has plenty of routes into hard knockdowns, enabling suffocating okizeme from various combos. He can even choose which side he wants his opponent on through the use of Raimei Gouha Nage, heavy Raimei Shin, or his supers. This means whenever he lands a hit, Geese can always put the opponent where he wants them.
  • Unpredictabo Mixups: Geese’s Fudou Ken is a swiss army knife of mix-up potential, enabling Geese to go overhead, low, or mid, while simultaneously creating stagger pressure with by holding and releasing all of the above options. He also has a command grab that he can whiff cancel into and a plus-on-block overhead in Heavy/REV Hishou Nichirin Zan. Thanks to his generous access to hard knockdowns, Geese can push this mix quite often.
  • Fireballin’: Just like his son, Rock's dead dad is great at safely harassing opponents from a distance with with Reppuu Ken and Shippuu Ken. He may even be better at it than Rock due to the absurd travel speed of REV Reppuu Ken and stricter downward angles of Shippuu Ken, making them better suited for standalone usage or punishing certain decisions from the opponent, along with the ability to feint both to bait opponents’ responses.
  • Terrible Defense: Geese has no meterless invincible reversals of any kind. His Atemi Nage counters, while fast at 3 frame startup, also leave him wide open if he fails to counter anything and will lose to properly timed meaties. Hishou Nichirin Zan, despite its DP input, has no invincibility and is not an actual reversal that can be used on wakeup or as an anti-air. Only his supers have invincibility, and they are naturally risky to use on defense.
  • Poor Anti-Airs: Geese doesn’t have a reliable anti-air, either. His High Dodge Attack’s pathetic hitbox makes baiting it to whiff quite easy, and his only answer against distant jumps is Double Reppuu Ken. Otherwise, he'll need to spend meter on Raging Storm or attempt a high counter, which will get him punished severely if the opponent does an empty jump.
  • Combo Inconsistency At Range: Geese has frustrating limitations on his mid-range combos. His routes into heavy Fudou Ken (214C) are range dependent, REV combos from the max range of 214C depend on the starter, and Double Reppuu Ken can be very picky to combo into depending on which lights are used. To ensure consistent combo routes, Geese may have to resort to his painfully slow far C, which also drops links into it at max range.
  • Bad Moveset Synergy: All of the above leads to one central, painful flaw: Geese’s moveset in CotW just isn’t cohesive. His strong fireballs have no good anti-air or long-range conversion to back them up, and his middling mobility and lack of approach tools outside of REV Blow restrict his access to his strong offense. As a result, all Geese has left to fill the holes in his gameplans are universal mechanics, all of which have substantial counterplay.

The pretzel input returns in all its glory. By performing the original Raging Storm input, Nightmare Geese performs a stronger version of the move.

  • This is a secret variation of Raging Storm and is not displayed on the command list in-game.
  • True Raging Storm does slightly more damage than the double quarter circle version of Raging Storm. It also has a slightly different visual and sound effect so you can tell the difference.
  • True Raging Storm can be comboed out of if it connects at a high enough height, around the apex of the opponent's jump height. This can usually be achieved by landing it as a precise anti-air or landing a Counter Hit special move that causes a launch state like Counter Hit Heavy Fudou Ken. After True Raging Storm connects in these contexts you can follow up with (REV) Raimei Gouha Nage or (REV) Raimei Shin and get some more out of them with REV Accel. This applies to the Redline Gear version as well, though as usual the only marginally better damage increase isn't worth spending a second bar in most cases. None of this will work on a normal Raging Storm, only the pretzel input version.
  • With all of that said, True Raging Storm is better than Raging Storm in pretty much every single way. There are a few use cases for the easy input version though; it's faster and easier of course, it might be better as a wakeup reversal since in that situation you would care more about getting the opponent off of you over getting slightly higher damage with a more complex input, you'd probably want to do the easier input version if the damage will end the round regardless of which input you use, and it's easier to use in Hyper Defense because of the forward inputs. Still, at least in combos you should always use True Raging Storm unless the input is too hard for you. It's only a very slight increase in damage and the followup combos require very specific situations. Don't worry if you can't pull it off.

Just like with Raging Storm, Nightmare Geese's Deadly Rave also has a stronger secret input version. In addition to the different motion input, the player has to manually input every hit in the ensuing combo after the initial lunge.

  • The True Deadly Rave button squence is as follows: 2141236B > A > A > B > B > B > C > C > C > 214C. If you're having trouble memorizing all of that, think of it as just two A presses, three B presses, three C presses, and then a quarter circle back + C press at the end. You can get away with just mashing the correct buttons as long as you know which hits in the animation are which.
  • True Deadly Rave does slightly more damage than the normal Deadly Rave.
  • The fact that every hit is a manual input means that the player can choose to stop the combo early, usually right before the 214C ender. Unlike prior games such as Garou: Mark of the Wolves or Capcom vs SNK 2 where stopping early leaves you with enough frame advantage to link a normal into a special move to get back some of your meter, here the only reason to end the combo before the final hit is to be +3 and right in front of the opponent, which compliments Nightmare Geese's strong offense. Losing out on more damage in the short term in exchange for the potential of more damage out of an ensuing mixup is usually worth it in most high level fighting game player's minds unless that short term damage is enough to win the round. Still, you get enough okizeme anyway that you can end it with the cool ki blast if you really want to.
  • As usual, there's no real reason to use the Redline Gear version unless the damage will win the round. It doesn't do much more damage than the Ignition Gear version to justify spending a valuable bar of super meter, on top of the fact that the True Deadly Rave combo does the same damage on both the Ignition and Redline versions before the ending hit, meaning there is no reason to use the Redline Gear version if you want to end the super early.
