Gameplay Overview

[edit]

(NOTE: This page is a work in progress.)

Chun-Li is the second guest character from Street Fighter to grace City of the Wolves. Though she sports her Street Fighter 6 look, this version of Chun-Li plays most like her infamous 3rd Strike version (albeit balanced better), while borrowing many different things from each past version of the character.

Like in 3rd Strike, Chun-Li is very well-rounded, but focuses on strong neutral control and footsies rather than raw aggression. She loves to fight in the mid-range, whittling away the opponent with her very strong normal attacks, both super- and special-cancellable. When the opponent opens themselves up, Chun-Li can use her great normals or her DP to turn one small mistake by her opponent into a ton of damage. Generous frame advantage on some normals really let her freestyle combos, using multiple links or REV Accel — however she wants to rack up her high damage, while also sporting a suite of powerful moves for mix-up potential, like a comboable overhead, low, or a Brake into a throw.

The biggest downside to Chun-Li in this game is that she lacks strong tools for hyper-aggression or zoning. Her only special moves that aren’t punishable on block are her REV fireball and REV legs, (when you Brake it). Not to mention, like Ken and Tizoc, Chun-Li cannot cancel her forward dash into a move, which strips away a layer of offense that the vast majority of the cast has access to. Of course, you can use REV Accel to push an offense, or turtle behind a fireball-DP neutral like a zoner, but Chun-Li really shines when she gets to play a proactive neutral game and out-space her adversary.

If the neutral game, overwhelming your opponent with tons of tools and confirming your way to victory sounds like your kind of character, you will definitely want to put the time into playing Chun-Li. Or if you want… just play her for kicks. Literally.

Chun-Li is an all-rounder who thrives in any situation, but is strongest at mid-range where she can poke and whittle down her opponent.
Pros Cons
  • Fantastic neutral: Chun-Li’s long pokes, great walk speed and access to both a projectile and DP make getting in on her a daunting task. Whiffing against her is also dangerous due to long-reaching Wild Punish starters such as her 6C.
  • Combo Machine: Chun-Li can effortlessly confirm into more powerful combos using powerful normal links, single-hit confirms, and her ability to cancel REV specials to supers.
  • Above Average Damage: Building on the last point, Chun-Li’s damage is unexpectedly high thanks to those same links and confirms resulting in combos scaling less than those that use REV specials, not to mention being able to use two supers in a combo. If Chun-Li has full resources, she can easily do 60% or more, with or without SPG.
  • Strong Mix-Up Potential: Meaty setups off of any hard knockdown, a command crossup in her 3B, an overhead that can be faked out, an overhead that combos on hit, and a cancellable low make Chun-Li’s offense and mixups not only versatile, but highly rewarding and often leading to vortex situations.
  • Slippery: Chun-Li is a difficult character to keep down, with her strong and fast DP, along with multiple tools to escape the corner like her wall jump and 3B.
  • Cannot Cancel Dash: Unlike most characters with a forward dash, Chun-Li’s dash cannot be cancelled into a move, making its use significantly more committal than other characters while taking away surprise throws and dash-up normals that other characters can do. She is thus forced to rely on her above-average walk speed to work her way in.
  • Bad Fireball: Chun-Li’s projectile is very slow to start up and recover compared to other characters, to the point of sometimes being punishable on hit. This heavily limits its use outside of the REV version.
  • LET’S GO JUSTIN!: Many of Chun-Li’s sequences on block, especially safe special cancels, are multiple hits, meaning Just Defense and Hyper Defense are especially effective against Chun-Li if her opponent knows the matchup. This can also make her more vulnerable to comebacks.
  • Technically Demanding: There is very little room for error with Chun-Li when it comes to execution, due to command normals with similar inputs yet wildly different functions, a reliance on linking, and to a lesser extent, input overlap. If you slip up and don’t get the move or combo you want, the mistake can reasonably lend to a round loss.


Guest from Metro City

[edit]

Chun-Li, much like Ken, is a guest character from Street Fighter, and as a result follows a few rules of that franchise, even in this game:

  • Chun-Li has no far or close variants of her normals, she gets the same ones regardless of proximity to the opponent.
  • Only one of Chun-Li’s standard heavy attacks has the usual Wild Punish effect and stagger, that being her 5D; however, several command normals also have the Wild Punish effect: 3C, 4C, 6C, 3D, and 6D. The rest of her normals apply standard Counter Hit effects on WP.
  • EX moves can be cancelled into supers on hit.
  • Neutral jumps and directional jumps result in different jump attacks for Chun-Li.
  • Chun-Li CANNOT cancel her dash into any actions; in exchange, her forward dash is much faster than other characters.
  • Additionally, Chun-Li has a large amount of command normals, and some of her regular normals are very unusual in this game, like a cancellable low poke in 2D.
